## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> The change in whole-body insulin sensitivity was correlated to the change in mitochondrial respiration.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 2)

The submission fails critical evidence verification. The citation trace for C007 shows a catastrophic alias match error: the term 'Mitochondrial' was resolved to 'MITOQUINONE MESYLATE'. Mitoquinone Mesylate is a specific pharmacological compound, not a general descriptor for mitochondrial protein synthesis. This indicates the evidence provided does not actually support the claim regarding mitochondrial protein synthesis rates during AET.

Furthermore, there is a complete absence of source abstracts or published results provided in the submission to verify the numeric or qualitative assertions in C004, C005, and C006. Without the source text to verify the 'attenuation' or 'correlation' claims, and given the hallucinated alias match in the trace, the evidence cannot be validated.

**Flagged claims:** C007

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission suffers from a critical 'off-topic drift' and a failure of domain specificity. The citation trace for C007 reveals that 'Mitochondrial' was resolved to 'MITOQUINONE MESYLATE'. Mitoquinone mesylate is a mitochondria-targeted antioxidant, which is a completely different pharmacological agent than Metformin. Using data from a study on Mitoquinone to support claims about Metformin's effect on mitochondrial protein synthesis is a fundamental error in evidence mapping.

Furthermore, the thesis (C006) claims a correlation between whole-body insulin sensitivity and mitochondrial respiration. While this may be observed in a specific study, the submission fails to provide the necessary caveats regarding the population (e.g., sedentary vs. athletic, human vs. rodent) or the specific type of aerobic exercise training (AET) used. Without these bounds, the claim is over-scoped and presented as a general mechanistic truth rather than a study-specific observation.

**Flagged claims:** C006, C007

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic in rejecting this submission. The most critical failure is the catastrophic alias match in the citation trace for C007, where the general term 'Mitochondrial' was incorrectly resolved to 'MITOQUINONE MESYLATE'. As noted by the Domain Skeptic, Mitoquinone Mesylate is a specific antioxidant compound, not a general descriptor of mitochondrial function, and is pharmacologically distinct from Metformin. This indicates that the evidence used to support the claims is fundamentally mismatched and invalid.

Additionally, the submission lacks the necessary context to support the thesis (C006). The claim of a correlation between insulin sensitivity and mitochondrial respiration is presented as a general mechanistic fact without specifying the model, population, or parameters of the aerobic exercise training (AET). Combined with the lack of source abstracts to verify the qualitative claims in C004 and C005, the submission fails to meet the basic requirements for evidence-based research.

**Flagged claims:** C006, C007
