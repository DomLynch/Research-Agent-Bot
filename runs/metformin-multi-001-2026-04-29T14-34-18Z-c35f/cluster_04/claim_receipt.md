## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

The submission fails fundamentally on evidence auditing grounds. While the citation traces claim to pass, there are no source abstracts provided in the input to verify the claims against. More critically, the claims (C015-C019) are written as a narrative excerpt from a paper (using first-person 'We describe' in C018), yet they are presented as research claims. 

Because there is no provided evidence text to verify the numeric or factual assertions (e.g., 'most commonly prescribed' in C015), and because the claims are essentially a verbatim copy of an introduction/abstract rather than synthesized findings supported by data, the evidence cannot be audited. In a strict audit, the absence of source text to validate the 'PASS' traces results in an automatic rejection.

**Flagged claims:** C015, C016, C017, C018, C019

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission suffers from severe mechanism inflation and a failure to address critical confounders. Specifically, C017 claims that metformin use in type II diabetes is associated with better survival than the general population. This is a classic example of 'healthy user bias' or 'selection bias'—patients prescribed metformin are often those actively managed for a chronic condition, and comparing them to the 'general population' without adjusting for the specific comorbidities of diabetes is scientifically unsound. 

Furthermore, C016 extrapolates findings from model organisms to human 'healthspan and lifespan' without sufficient caveats regarding the translational gap. While the thesis (C018) claims to describe limitations, the supporting claims (C016, C017) present these speculative or biased associations as established mechanistic benefits. The scope of the claims is not appropriately calibrated to the strength of the evidence, framing observational correlations as promising pharmacological interventions for aging.

**Flagged claims:** C016, C017

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission is fundamentally flawed because it presents a narrative excerpt from a specific paper (evidenced by the first-person 'We describe' in C018) as a set of research claims, rather than synthesizing evidence to support a thesis. This structural failure makes the claims descriptive of a paper's intent rather than assertions of scientific fact.

Furthermore, I find the Domain Skeptic's point regarding C017 particularly damning. The claim that metformin users have better survival than the 'general population' is a textbook example of selection bias that should not be presented as a mechanistic benefit without rigorous qualification. The lack of provided source text to verify the 'PASS' traces, as noted by the Auditor, leaves the panel with no way to validate the claims beyond the provided text, which is internally inconsistent in its framing.

**Flagged claims:** C015, C016, C017, C018, C019
