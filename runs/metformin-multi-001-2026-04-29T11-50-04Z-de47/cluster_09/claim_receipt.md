## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission fails the evidence audit due to multiple failed citation traces. Specifically, critical aliases used in claims C022 and C023 ('Young' and 'Transcriptomic') failed to resolve, indicating a drift between the claim assertions and the provided evidence. In a research review, the inability to trace key comparative groups (Young vs. Old) and methodology (Transcriptomic analyses) constitutes a failure of evidence support.

**Flagged claims:** C022, C023

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission suffers from severe mechanism inflation and over-claiming. The thesis (C025) asserts that a mere 2-week ingestion period induced 'persistent effects' and 'chromatin remodeling.' However, the evidence provided is based on in vitro performance of extracted cells. There is a massive leap in logic to claim that short-term ingestion creates a 'persistent' epigenetic shift without longitudinal data showing these effects endure after the drug is cleared from the system.

Furthermore, the claims are contradictory and poorly scoped. C024 admits that metformin 'exacerbated pathways related to inflammation signaling,' yet the thesis frames the overall outcome as an 'improvement' in function. The submission fails to reconcile how an increase in inflammatory signaling aligns with a functional improvement in muscle progenitor cells (MPCs), hiding a significant biological confounder.

Finally, the reliance on a single study [11] to claim systemic chromatin remodeling in a specific cell population based on short-term administration is an over-extrapolation of mechanistic data.

**Flagged claims:** C024, C025

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission is fundamentally flawed on two levels: evidentiary traceability and biological plausibility. The Auditor correctly identified that the core comparative framework (Young vs. Old) and the methodology (Transcriptomics) failed the citation trace, meaning the claims are not rigorously anchored to the source text. 

More critically, I find the Domain Skeptic's analysis of 'mechanism inflation' to be decisive. Claiming 'persistent effects' and 'chromatin remodeling' from a 2-week administration based on in vitro results is a significant over-extrapolation. Furthermore, the submission presents a contradictory narrative where the drug simultaneously improves function (C025) and exacerbates inflammatory signaling (C024) without providing any synthesis or reconciliation of these opposing effects. The reliance on a single reference to support such broad epigenetic claims is insufficient for a research-grade thesis.

**Flagged claims:** C022, C023, C024, C025
