# Within diabetics, the strong boosting effect of the high-dose vaccine was significantly dampened for metformin users (n = 64) as compared to non-users (n = 30), but still remained slightly higher than standard-dose responses

**SPAR verdict:** `accept_caveated` (2-1 accept).

**Dissent — `domain_skeptic`:** The submission suffers from significant sample size issues and a lack of necessary caveats regarding the nature of the association. The total sample size for the diabetic subgroup is extremely small (n=94), with only 30 non-users serving as the control group. In a clinical context, such a small cohort is highly susceptible to selection bias and confounders—such as the severity of the diabetes, comorbidities, or concurrent medications—which are not addressed in the claims. 

Furthermore, C058 frames the result as an association ('metformin may be associated with reductions'), but the preceding claims present the 'dampening effect' as a direct consequence of the drug. Given that this is likely an observational analysis within a larger trial rather than a randomized controlled trial specifically designed to test metformin's effect on vaccine response, the leap from a statistical p-value in a tiny subgroup to a generalizable clinical suggestion is over-scoped.
