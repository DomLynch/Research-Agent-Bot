## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> Metformin also significantly improved arterial stiffness and alleviated structural changes in aged arteries, reduced senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), and improved proliferation and migration of senescent VSMCs.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

The submission is fundamentally flawed as it provides no source abstracts or published results for the cited reference [3]. As the Evidence Auditor, I cannot verify if the claims regarding arterial stiffness, SASP, autophagic flux, or autolysosome formation are supported because the evidence itself is missing from the provided data. All claims are marked as 'mechanistic' but lack the necessary source text to validate the trace.

**Flagged claims:** C003, C004, C005, C006, C007

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission suffers from severe mechanism inflation and over-claiming. The entire thesis and all supporting claims rely on a single reference [3], which describes effects in 'VSMCs' (Vascular Smooth Muscle Cells) and 'vascular tissues' under 'Ang II-dependent' (Angiotensin II) conditions. This is a highly specific preclinical/experimental model of induced senescence, yet the thesis frames these as improvements in 'aged arteries' and 'structural changes,' implying a general anti-aging or therapeutic effect in aged populations that is not supported by the narrow scope of the evidence provided.

Furthermore, the leap from observing autophagosome-lysosome fusion in a cell culture or animal model to claiming that metformin 'prevents' vascular senescence (C007) is an over-extension. The evidence is mechanistic and preliminary, but the claims are phrased as definitive outcomes. There is no mention of whether these results translate to human clinical outcomes or if they are limited to a specific animal model, creating a significant gap between the experimental data and the broad conclusions drawn.

**Flagged claims:** C004, C007

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission is critically deficient because it relies entirely on a single reference [3] for which no source text or abstract was provided, making independent verification of the mechanistic claims impossible. This lack of transparency is a fatal flaw in a research review.

Furthermore, I find the Domain Skeptic's critique regarding 'mechanism inflation' highly persuasive. The claims extrapolate findings from a specific, induced model (Ang II-dependent senescence in VSMCs) to broad conclusions about 'aged arteries' and the 'prevention' of vascular senescence. This leap from a narrow experimental condition to a general therapeutic effect is an over-extension of the data. Given the absence of the source text and the over-reaching nature of the thesis, the submission cannot be accepted.

**Flagged claims:** C003, C004, C005, C006, C007
