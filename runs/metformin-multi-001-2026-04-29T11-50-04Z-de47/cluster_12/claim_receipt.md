### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission suffers from severe mechanism inflation and over-claiming. The evidence provided is based on a single study (Ref 7) involving aged mice. However, the thesis (C016) frames the results as a 'new strategy to prevent ovarian fibrosis' without any qualification regarding the species or the preclinical nature of the data. Extrapolating findings from a mouse model to a general 'strategy' for preventing a clinical condition in humans is an unacceptable leap in scope.

Furthermore, the claims (C014, C015) describe the modulation of cell populations as the mechanism of prevention, but the evidence is purely correlative/observational within a single animal model. There is no evidence provided to suggest these effects are reproducible across different populations or that they translate to human ovarian physiology. The lack of caveats regarding sample size, species specificity, and the gap between murine 'modulation' and human 'prevention' makes the conclusion over-scoped.

**Flagged claims:** C014, C015, C016
