# Submission rejected

## DRAFT REJECTED

**SPAR verdict:** `reject_critical` (3-0 unanimous reject).

## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> metformin use was significantly associated with greater AD-CT and better global cognition among APOE4 non-carriers, but not among carriers.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

The submission fails the evidence audit because it provides no source abstracts or published results for the cited reference [24]. As the Evidence Auditor, I cannot verify if the numeric claims, the specific associations with AD-CT, or the interaction with APOE4 status actually appear in the source text. Claims marked as 'direct' or 'mechanistic' that lack a verifiable published_results citation or corresponding abstract text are considered unsupported.

**Flagged claims:** C048, C049, C050

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission presents an association between metformin use and cortical thickness (AD-CT) and cognition as a mechanistic finding, which is a significant over-claim. Observational associations in a specific population do not constitute a 'mechanism.' Furthermore, the thesis (C050) frames these results as a definitive interaction based on a single reference, without addressing the inherent confounders of metformin use (e.g., diabetes status, BMI, and cardiovascular comorbidities) which are heavily intertwined with both APOE4 status and cognitive decline.

There is a clear issue of over-scoping here: the claims present a binary outcome (works for non-carriers, not for carriers) without providing the necessary caveats regarding sample size or the strength of the interaction. By labeling these as 'mechanistic' claims, the author is inflating a correlational observation into a causal or biological rule, which is an unacceptable extrapolation of the evidence provided.

**Flagged claims:** C048, C049, C050

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission suffers from a fundamental failure of verification; without the source text for reference [24], the specific claims regarding AD-CT and APOE4 interactions are entirely unsubstantiated. Even if the data were present, the Domain Skeptic correctly identifies a critical category error: labeling observational associations as 'mechanistic' claims. This is a significant over-reach that misrepresents the nature of the evidence.

Because both prior reviewers identified independent and severe flaws—one regarding the absence of verifiable evidence and the other regarding the conceptual misclassification of the findings—there is no basis for acceptance. The lack of transparency regarding confounders (diabetes, BMI) further weakens the validity of the thesis.

**Flagged claims:** C048, C049, C050
