### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission presents a sweeping, universal negative conclusion ('was not associated') based on a single reference. This is a classic case of over-claiming by failing to scope the finding to a specific population, dosage, duration of treatment, or specific neurocognitive domain. Without specifying whether this refers to a specific clinical trial, a meta-analysis, or a particular patient cohort (e.g., T2DM vs. non-diabetic), the claim is an over-generalization that ignores the heterogeneity of metformin research.

Furthermore, the claim lacks necessary caveats regarding the study design of the supporting evidence. A single study showing 'no association' does not support a general thesis of non-association across the domain, especially given the known variability in neurocognitive endpoints. The conclusion is inappropriately scoped and lacks the calibration required for a domain-expert review.

**Flagged claims:** C026

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission fails on two fundamental levels: verification and scoping. First, the absence of the source text for citation [12] makes it impossible to verify the claim's accuracy, rendering the 'direct' support label baseless. Second, even if the source were present, the claim is phrased as a universal negative, which is scientifically unsound when based on a single reference. The lack of population parameters, dosage, or specific cognitive endpoints transforms a potential study finding into an over-generalized conclusion.

Because both previous reviewers identified critical failures—one regarding the integrity of the evidence trail and the other regarding the scientific validity of the claim's scope—there is no basis for acceptance. The submission lacks the necessary rigor and transparency required for a research claim.
