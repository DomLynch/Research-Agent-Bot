# CT scan showed that increases in thigh muscle area (p = .005) and density (p = .020) were greater in placebo versus metformin

**SPAR verdict:** `accept_caveated` (2-1 accept).

**Dissent — `domain_skeptic`:** The submission suffers from significant mechanism inflation and over-claiming in its concluding statement. While claims C030 through C033 report specific data points from a single study (NCT02308228), claim C034 transforms these specific findings into a broad, definitive conclusion that metformin 'negatively impacts the hypertrophic response.' This is an over-reach; a single study in a specific population (healthy older individuals) does not justify a generalized claim of negative impact without acknowledging the nuances of dosage, duration, and the specific nature of the 'blunted' response mentioned in C033.

Furthermore, C033 describes a 'trend' for blunted mTORC1 signaling. In clinical research, a 'trend' is statistically non-significant. Framing this trend as part of a direct evidence chain to support the definitive conclusion in C034 is a failure of calibration. The submission presents a mechanistic hypothesis as a proven outcome.

## Thesis

CT scan showed that increases in thigh muscle area (p = .005) and density (p = .020) were greater in placebo versus metformin [15].

## Findings

placebo gained more lean body mass (p = .003) and thigh muscle mass (p < .001) than metformin [15].
placebo had decreased type I fiber percentage while metformin did not (p = .007) [15].
Metformin led to an increase in AMPK signaling, and a trend for blunted increases in mTORC1 signaling in response to PRT [15].
metformin negatively impacts the hypertrophic response to resistance training in healthy older individuals [15].

## References

[15] Metformin blunts muscle hypertrophy in response to progressive resistance exercise training in older adults: A randomized, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled, multicenter trial: The MASTERS trial (2019). https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.13039
