# Submission rejected

## DRAFT REJECTED

**SPAR verdict:** `reject_critical` (3-0 unanimous reject).

## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> Fasting or treatment with metformin can reverse these changes and restore the regenerative capacity of aged OPCs, improving remyelination in aged animals following focal demyelination.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

The submission is fundamentally flawed as it provides no source abstracts or published results for the cited reference [4]. As the Evidence Auditor, I cannot verify if the claims regarding the regenerative capacity of aged OPCs or the responsiveness to pro-differentiation signals are supported by the evidence because the evidence itself is missing from the provided context. Furthermore, there are no trace results for 'p_value_in_text' or 'percentage_in_text' to validate the mechanistic claims.

**Flagged claims:** C008, C009

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission suffers from severe mechanism inflation and over-claiming. Claim C008 asserts that metformin can 'reverse' aging changes and 'restore' regenerative capacity to improve remyelination in aged animals. This is a high-level functional claim based on a single reference, likely from a preclinical animal model, yet it is framed as a definitive restorative effect without any mentioned caveats regarding species specificity or the transient nature of such interventions. 

Furthermore, C009 extrapolates these findings to suggest 'synergistic effects' with pro-differentiation therapies. This is a speculative leap; observing that a cell 'regains responsiveness' is not evidence of a synergistic therapeutic effect in a clinical or systemic context. The claims are over-scoped, transforming a specific mechanistic observation into a broad therapeutic conclusion without accounting for the gap between aged animal models and human pathology.

**Flagged claims:** C008, C009

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission fails on two critical fronts: evidentiary transparency and scientific rigor. First, as noted by the Auditor, the absence of the actual source text for reference [4] makes the claims unverifiable, rendering the submission an assertion rather than a research-backed claim. Second, the Skeptic correctly identifies a pattern of 'mechanism inflation.' The transition from a cellular observation (regained responsiveness) to a broad therapeutic conclusion (synergistic effects and restoration of regenerative capacity) is a speculative leap that is not supported by the provided data. Even if the source text were present, the phrasing 'reverse these changes' is an over-claim for what is typically a modulation of metabolic pathways in preclinical models.

**Flagged claims:** C008, C009
