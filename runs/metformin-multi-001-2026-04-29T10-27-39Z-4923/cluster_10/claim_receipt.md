# Submission rejected

## DRAFT REJECTED

**SPAR verdict:** `reject_critical` (3-0 unanimous reject).

## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> Metformin treatment has been shown to improve insulin sensitivity and attenuate muscle loss in insulin resistance adults though the mechanisms are not fully known.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

Both claims C073 and C074 are marked as 'direct' claims, which requires a citation to published results. However, the only supporting reference provided, Ref [34] (NCT03107884), is a clinical trial that is listed as 'active_not_recruiting' with 'has_results=False'. 

Because the cited evidence contains no published results, it cannot support a direct claim regarding the effects of metformin on insulin sensitivity or muscle loss (C073), nor can it serve as a basis for a statement on the current state of investigation (C074). The evidence fails the requirement for published_results citations for direct claims.

**Flagged claims:** C073, C074

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 2)

The submission suffers from severe over-claiming and a fundamental failure to calibrate conclusions based on the provided evidence. Claim C073 asserts that metformin 'has been shown to improve' insulin sensitivity and 'attenuate muscle loss' in adults with insulin resistance. However, the only supporting reference provided (NCT03107884) is a clinical trial that is listed as 'active_not_recruiting' with no results reported. It is logically impossible to claim a treatment 'has been shown' to achieve a specific outcome based on a trial that has not yet published its results.

Furthermore, the claim regarding the attenuation of muscle loss is highly contentious in the domain of exercise physiology and endocrinology, as some evidence suggests metformin may actually blunt hypertrophic responses to resistance training. By framing a pending trial as a proven outcome, the submission engages in mechanism inflation and ignores the lack of empirical data.

**Flagged claims:** C073

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 1)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission commits a fundamental error in evidence attribution: it cites a clinical trial (NCT03107884) that has not yet published results to support a claim that an effect 'has been shown.' A pending trial cannot serve as empirical proof of a therapeutic outcome. 

Furthermore, the Domain Skeptic correctly notes that the claim regarding the attenuation of muscle loss is scientifically contentious, making the lack of a valid, peer-reviewed citation even more critical. Because the only provided reference is an empty shell of a trial, neither the positive claim (C073) nor the gap-analysis claim (C074) has any evidentiary basis. The submission is rejected for total lack of supporting data.

**Flagged claims:** C073, C074
