## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> within diabetics, the strong boosting effect of the high-dose vaccine was significantly dampened for metformin users (n = 64) as compared to non-users (n = 30), but still remained slightly higher than standard-dose responses.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 3)

Claim C057 contains specific numeric values (n = 64 and n = 30) that are not verified by the provided citation traces. While the p-values for C058 and C059 were successfully traced to the source abstract, there is no trace confirming the existence of these sample sizes in the source text. As an Evidence Auditor, I must reject any submission where numeric values in claims are not explicitly supported by the evidence traces.

**Flagged claims:** C057

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 4)

The submission presents these findings as a generalizable 'dampening effect' of metformin on vaccine response, but the sample sizes provided (n=64 for users, n=30 for non-users) are critically small for a population as heterogeneous as diabetics. The submission fails to mention essential confounders that typically plague such small-cohort observational data, such as the duration of diabetes, HbA1c levels (glycemic control), or concomitant medications, all of which independently influence vaccine immunogenicity. 

Furthermore, the claims frame the result as a direct effect of metformin, whereas in a cohort of this size, the observed difference could easily be driven by a few outliers or baseline differences in the health status of the two groups. By presenting p-values without the context of the small sample size and lack of controlled variables, the submission over-scopes a preliminary observation into a definitive mechanistic conclusion.

**Flagged claims:** C057, C058, C059

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 3)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The Evidence Auditor correctly identifies a critical failure in the audit trail: the specific sample sizes (n=64, n=30) cited in the thesis (C057) were not verified by the citation traces. In a research review, the presence of unverified numeric data is a primary ground for rejection, as it suggests a lack of rigorous evidence mapping.

Furthermore, I find the Domain Skeptic's critique regarding the clinical interpretation to be compelling. The submission presents a 'significant dampening effect' based on a very small, unbalanced cohort without addressing confounding variables (such as HbA1c levels) that are standard in diabetic immunogenicity studies. The transition from a small-sample observation to a definitive claim about metformin's effect is an overreach. Because the submission fails both on the technical grounds of evidence verification and the scientific grounds of clinical validity, I vote to reject.

**Flagged claims:** C057, C058, C059
