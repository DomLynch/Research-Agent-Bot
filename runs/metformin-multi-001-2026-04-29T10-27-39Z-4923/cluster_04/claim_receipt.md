### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission presents data from a study with an extremely small sample size (n=40 total), yet frames the results as direct evidence without any caveats regarding the lack of statistical power. Specifically, C060 reports a non-significant result (p=0.627) with a wide confidence interval, which in a sample this small is an underpowered observation rather than a definitive finding of 'no difference.'

More critically, C061 and C062 present safety data from a tiny cohort (19-21 people) as a basis for safety claims. Extrapolating the absence of serious adverse events from 19 participants to a general conclusion about metformin's safety profile is a classic case of mechanism/outcome inflation. The submission fails to scope these findings as a small-scale pilot or preliminary observation, presenting them instead as absolute data points.

**Flagged claims:** C060, C061, C062

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 3)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The Evidence Auditor correctly identified a technical failure in the citation trace for C060, where the term 'PhenoAge' was not resolved, creating a gap in the evidentiary chain. More importantly, I find the Domain Skeptic's critique regarding the sample size and framing to be decisive. Presenting safety data (C061, C062) and efficacy outcomes (C060) from a cohort of only 40 people as direct, uncontextualized claims is misleading. The wide confidence intervals and high p-value in C060 indicate a lack of statistical power that the submission fails to acknowledge. Because the submission presents these preliminary, underpowered results as absolute facts without scoping them as a small-scale pilot, it fails the standard for research integrity.

**Flagged claims:** C060, C061, C062
