# Submission rejected

## DRAFT REJECTED

**SPAR verdict:** `reject_critical` (3-0 unanimous reject).

## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> metformin use was significantly associated with greater AD-CT and better global cognition among APOE4 non-carriers, but not among carriers.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission fails the evidence audit because it lacks the necessary source abstracts to verify the claims. While the role_match traces are marked as 'PASS', there is no provided text from reference [24] to confirm that the specific findings regarding AD-CT, Aβ, tau, WMH volume, and the APOE4 interaction actually exist in the source. Without the source abstracts, the numeric and qualitative assertions in C050, C051, and C052 are completely unsupported.

**Flagged claims:** C050, C051, C052

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission relies entirely on a single reference [24] to support three distinct claims, including a complex interaction effect between a drug, a genetic marker (APOE4), and cognitive outcomes. The claims are framed as significant associations, yet the evidence is labeled as 'low' strength. In the context of neurodegenerative research, associating a drug with 'better global cognition' based on a single study—especially when the effect is limited to a specific genetic subgroup—is a classic case of over-claiming without providing the necessary caveats regarding sample size, study design (observational vs. interventional), or the potential for confounding variables common in metformin users (e.g., metabolic syndrome, diabetes management).

**Flagged claims:** C052

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission suffers from a critical failure in transparency and evidentiary depth. First, as noted by the Auditor, there is a complete absence of source text for reference [24], meaning the claims are essentially assertions without verifiable proof. Second, the Domain Skeptic correctly identifies the high risk of over-claiming; attributing cognitive benefits to metformin based on a single study—particularly one with 'low' strength evidence and a complex genetic interaction—is scientifically premature and lacks the necessary contextual caveats regarding study design and confounding variables.

Because both prior reviewers identified fundamental flaws—one regarding the lack of verifiable data and the other regarding the fragility of the scientific conclusion—there is no basis for acceptance. The reliance on a single, unprovided source to support a specific genetic interaction effect is insufficient for a research-grade claim.

**Flagged claims:** C050, C051, C052
