> Fasting or treatment with metformin can reverse these changes and restore the regenerative capacity of aged OPCs, improving remyelination in aged animals following focal demyelination.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

The submission fails fundamentally on evidence verification. While the citation traces indicate a 'PASS' for role and alias matching, there are no provided source abstracts or published results to verify the actual content of the claims. As the Evidence Auditor, I cannot verify if the claims regarding 'regenerative capacity of aged OPCs' or 'responsiveness to pro-differentiation signals' are actually supported by the cited reference [4] because the source text is missing from the input. Furthermore, the claims are marked as 'mechanistic' (direct), yet there is no evidence of a published_results citation being validated against a source text.

**Flagged claims:** C009, C010

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission suffers from severe mechanism inflation and over-claiming. Claim C009 asserts that metformin can 'reverse' aging changes and 'restore' regenerative capacity in aged OPCs to improve remyelination. This is a strong causal claim of rejuvenation that is typically based on preclinical animal models (as indicated by the mention of 'aged animals'), yet it is presented as a definitive functional outcome without specifying the species or the limitations of the model. 

Furthermore, C010 extrapolates these findings to suggest 'synergistic effects' for future therapies. This is a speculative leap from a mechanistic observation to a clinical implication. The submission fails to provide any caveats regarding the translation from animal models to human pathology, nor does it address potential confounders such as the dosage of metformin or the specific type of focal demyelination used. The scope of the conclusion far exceeds the strength of the provided mechanistic evidence.

**Flagged claims:** C009, C010

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission is fundamentally flawed due to a total lack of verifiable source text for reference [4], making it impossible to determine if the claims are grounded in actual data or are entirely fabricated. Even if the reference existed, the Domain Skeptic correctly identifies that the language used ('reverse', 'restore') constitutes an over-claim of causal rejuvenation that lacks the necessary nuance, species-specification, and translational caveats required for a mechanistic claim of this magnitude.

Because both previous reviewers identified critical failures—one on the grounds of evidentiary void and the other on the grounds of scientific over-extrapolation—there is no basis for acceptance. The lack of source material is a fatal flaw that precludes any further validation of the claims.

**Flagged claims:** C009, C010
