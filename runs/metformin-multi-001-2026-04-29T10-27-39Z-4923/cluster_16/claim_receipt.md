### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission suffers from significant extrapolation and a lack of calibration regarding the evidence's domain. The claims rely on a single source [11] describing in vitro performance of Muscle Progenitor Cells (MPCs) extracted from subjects. However, the thesis and claims frame these as 'persistent effects' and functional improvements without acknowledging the critical gap between in vitro cellular behavior (fusion index, homing) and actual in vivo clinical outcomes in old subjects. 

Furthermore, there is a glaring contradiction and lack of nuance between C024/C027 (which claim functional improvement) and C026 (which notes that metformin exacerbated inflammation signaling). The submission fails to reconcile how a drug that increases inflammatory pathways simultaneously 'improves' cellular function, suggesting a cherry-picking of positive outcomes while ignoring the potential negative mechanistic trade-offs. The use of unresolved aliases ('Young') and the leap from short-term ingestion to 'persistent' transcriptional signatures without defining the duration of persistence in a biological context constitutes over-claiming.

**Flagged claims:** C024, C026, C027

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission is fundamentally flawed due to a combination of technical trace failures and conceptual over-reach. The Auditor correctly identified that key comparative terms ('Young', 'Transcriptomic') failed alias matching, which suggests the claims are not precisely mapped to the source text. More critically, I find the Domain Skeptic's analysis of the mechanistic contradictions compelling: the submission claims functional improvement (C024, C027) while simultaneously admitting to the exacerbation of inflammatory signaling (C026) without providing any synthesis or reconciliation of these opposing effects. This indicates a fragmented representation of the source material that obscures the actual biological outcome. Finally, the leap from in vitro cellular metrics to 'persistent effects' on the organism's progenitor cells is an unsupported extrapolation that renders the thesis scientifically imprecise.

**Flagged claims:** C024, C025, C026, C027
