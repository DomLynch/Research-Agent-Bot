# Submission rejected

## DRAFT REJECTED

**SPAR verdict:** `reject_critical` (3-0 unanimous reject).

## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> The objective of this research is to assess the efficacy of oral metformin in mitigating the aging process in middle-aged and elderly males, to pinpoint sensitive indicators of human senescence, and to offer innovative frameworks and scientific insights for pharmaceutical interventions in aging.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

Claim C075 is marked as 'direct', which requires a published_results citation. However, the citation trace for reference [38] (NCT06459310) explicitly states that the study status is 'recruiting' and 'has_results=False'. A study that has not yet published results cannot provide evidence for a claim of efficacy or research outcomes, and it fails the requirement for a published_results citation for direct claims.

**Flagged claims:** C075

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 2)

The submission claims to 'assess the efficacy' of metformin in mitigating aging, yet the supporting evidence (NCT06459310) is for a study that is currently 'recruiting' and has no results. It is scientifically impossible to assess efficacy or pinpoint indicators of senescence based on a trial that has not yet produced data. This is a textbook case of over-claiming where the existence of a study protocol is being conflated with the achievement of the study's objectives.

Furthermore, the thesis frames these goals as research outcomes rather than research intentions. By stating the objective is to 'offer innovative frameworks' based on this specific trial, the author is extrapolating a conclusion before the evidence has even been gathered. The claim is entirely unsupported by the current state of the cited reference.

**Flagged claims:** C075

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 1)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission attempts to present the objectives of a clinical trial as a basis for a research claim, but the cited reference (NCT06459310) is currently in the recruiting phase with no results published. A study protocol cannot serve as evidence for the efficacy of an intervention or the identification of senescence indicators.

The author has conflated the 'intent' of a study with the 'outcome' of research. Because the evidence is non-existent at this stage, the claim is fundamentally unsupported. There is no dissent to weigh in this instance, as both prior reviewers correctly identified the critical failure of the citation trace.

**Flagged claims:** C075
