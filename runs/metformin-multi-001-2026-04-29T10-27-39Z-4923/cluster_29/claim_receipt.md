This is a classic case of over-claiming and mechanism inflation. 'Effectively delays' is a strong clinical conclusion that requires robust, longitudinal data across diverse primate cohorts to account for individual variability and potential confounders. 

Furthermore, the claim fails to specify the primate species, the duration of 'long-term' administration, or the specific biomarkers used to define 'cochlear aging.' Without these caveats, the claim extrapolates a potentially narrow experimental result into a generalized biological fact. The evidence provided is insufficient to support such a broad conclusion regarding primate physiology.
