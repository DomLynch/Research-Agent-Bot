## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `accept` (score 10)

All claims are supported by the provided citation traces. Specifically, the numeric values in C064 (49.6% and p > 0.05) were successfully verified against the source abstract. ### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission suffers from significant mechanism inflation and a failure to account for the primary confounder in this domain: glycemic control. Claim C065 frames the 'protective effect' on cognitive function as something that occurs 'not only' via glycemic improvement, implying a direct neuroprotective mechanism. However, the provided evidence is based on a meta-analysis of patients with diabetes; it is impossible to decouple the cognitive benefits of metformin from the cognitive benefits of stabilizing blood glucose levels without a controlled comparison against other glucose-lowering agents. 

Furthermore, Claim C064 misrepresents statistical metrics. A heterogeneity value of 49.6% (likely I²) is generally considered moderate to substantial, not 'low,' and citing a p-value > 0.05 as a 'low risk of bias' is a fundamental misunderstanding of statistical significance versus methodological bias. The claims are over-scoped and fail to calibrate the results against the inherent confounding variables of the patient population.

**Flagged claims:** C063, C064, C065

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 4)

I am breaking the tie in favor of the Domain Skeptic. While the Evidence Auditor correctly noted that the text of the claims matches the text of the source, the Domain Skeptic correctly identified critical failures in the interpretation of the data. Specifically, C064 describes a heterogeneity of 49.6% as 'low,' which is statistically inaccurate (typically categorized as moderate), and erroneously equates a p-value > 0.05 with a 'low risk of bias,' which is a category error in statistical reporting. 

Furthermore, C065 makes a causal claim regarding a 'protective effect' that is distinct from glycemic improvement. Without evidence comparing metformin to other glucose-lowering agents, this claim constitutes an over-reach. The submission prioritizes literal string-matching over scientific validity, leading to a misrepresentation of the evidence's strength and meaning.

**Flagged claims:** C063, C064, C065
