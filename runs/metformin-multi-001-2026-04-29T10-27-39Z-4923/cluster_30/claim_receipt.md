# Submission rejected

## DRAFT REJECTED

**SPAR verdict:** `reject_critical` (3-0 unanimous reject).

## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> allow immediate progress on the design of the next Targeting Aging with MEtformin (TAME)-like study involving such candidate gerotherapeutics.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

The submission contains a critical failure in the citation trace. The alias match for claim C044 resolves 'Targeting' (referring to the TAME study) to 'VIBOSTOLIMAB'. VIBOSTOLIMAB is a monoclonal antibody, whereas the TAME study specifically investigates Metformin. This is a fundamental mismatch between the claim's subject and the evidence provided. Furthermore, the claim is a prescriptive statement about study design rather than a factual finding supported by the cited evidence.

**Flagged claims:** C044

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 2)

The claim is a textbook example of mechanism inflation and over-claiming. It suggests that the evidence provided allows for 'immediate progress' on the design of a TAME-like study (a massive, multi-year human clinical trial for aging) based on what appears to be a single reference. Furthermore, the citation trace reveals a critical 'off-topic drift': the alias match indicates that the evidence actually refers to 'VIBOSTOLIMAB', not metformin. Extrapolating results from a different drug to justify the design of a metformin-centric aging study is scientifically unsound.

Additionally, the claim frames a mechanistic finding as a direct justification for a large-scale clinical trial design without addressing the massive gap between preclinical/mechanistic data and the rigorous requirements of a TAME-like study. The scope is wildly over-extended.

**Flagged claims:** C044

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 1)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission suffers from a catastrophic failure in evidence mapping: the citation trace reveals that the evidence provided refers to VIBOSTOLIMAB, a monoclonal antibody, while the claim concerns the TAME study, which is specifically focused on Metformin. This is not a minor discrepancy but a fundamental mismatch of the pharmacological agent being discussed.

Furthermore, the claim is an over-extension of the data. Suggesting that a single reference (which is off-topic) allows for 'immediate progress' on the design of a massive clinical trial like TAME is scientifically irresponsible. The gap between the provided evidence and the prescriptive conclusion is insurmountable.

**Flagged claims:** C044
