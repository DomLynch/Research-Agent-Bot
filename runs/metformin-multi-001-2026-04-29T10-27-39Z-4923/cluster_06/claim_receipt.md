### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission presents a sweeping negative conclusion ('not associated with improved neurocognitive outcomes') based on a single reference. This is a classic case of over-scoping. Neurocognitive outcomes are highly heterogeneous, encompassing memory, executive function, and processing speed, and can be influenced by a multitude of confounders such as baseline glycemic control, duration of diabetes, and comorbidities. 

By framing the result as a general lack of association without specifying the population, the specific cognitive domains measured, or the study design of reference [12], the claim ignores the likelihood of population specificity or trial-specific limitations. A single study cannot support a generalized negative thesis for a drug as widely studied as metformin across diverse neurocognitive contexts.

**Flagged claims:** C028

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 1)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. There is no evidence provided to justify such a broad negative conclusion.
