## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> randomized interventional trials in metabolically healthy older adults indicate that metformin can blunt resistance exercise-induced muscle hypertrophy and protein synthesis, likely through sustained activation of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) and consequent suppression of mammalian target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) signaling.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 2)

The submission fails fundamentally on evidence provision. While the citation traces claim a 'PASS' for role matching, there are no source abstracts or published results provided for reference [25] in the input. As the Evidence Auditor, I cannot verify if the claims regarding muscle hypertrophy blunting, AMPK activation, or mTORC1 suppression are actually supported by the cited text. Furthermore, the claims are marked as 'mechanistic' but lack the necessary published_results citations required for direct evidence verification.

**Flagged claims:** C053, C054, C055, C056

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 4)

The submission suffers from significant mechanism inflation and over-claiming. Specifically, C055 frames the blunting of muscle hypertrophy as a settled conclusion derived from 'randomized interventional trials,' yet it attributes this effect to a specific molecular pathway (AMPK/mTORC1) using the word 'likely.' In human interventional trials, measuring the 'sustained activation' of AMPK and the 'consequent suppression' of mTORC1 in vivo during exercise is notoriously difficult and often relies on proxy markers or small-sample biopsies that do not definitively prove a causal chain. The claim presents a mechanistic hypothesis as a factual outcome of the trials.

Furthermore, C056 extrapolates these findings into a prescriptive dichotomy—suggesting metformin 'may provide metabolic protection' for some while 'limiting anabolic adaptations' for others. This creates a clinical narrative that is not sufficiently supported by the provided evidence, which is listed as 'low' confidence. The jump from 'low' confidence mechanistic observations to a generalized conclusion about frail vs. active populations is an over-reach in scoping.

**Flagged claims:** C055, C056

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission is fundamentally flawed due to a complete lack of verifiable source material; reference [25] is cited across all claims, but no abstract or text was provided to validate the assertions. This makes the 'PASS' in the citation trace meaningless, as there was no content to match against.

Furthermore, I find the Domain Skeptic's critique of C055 and C056 highly persuasive. The thesis attempts to bridge a gap between clinical outcomes (blunted hypertrophy) and molecular mechanisms (AMPK/mTORC1) with a level of certainty that is rarely supported in human interventional trials. The transition from 'low' confidence mechanistic claims to a broad clinical dichotomy regarding frail versus active populations is an unjustified leap in logic. Given the absence of evidence and the presence of mechanism inflation, the submission cannot be accepted.

**Flagged claims:** C053, C054, C055, C056
