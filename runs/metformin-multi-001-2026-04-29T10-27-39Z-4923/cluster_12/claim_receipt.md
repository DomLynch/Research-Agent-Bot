# Submission rejected

## DRAFT REJECTED

**SPAR verdict:** `reject_critical` (3-0 unanimous reject).

## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> This study aims to assess the safety and efficacy of study drugs and supplements on clinical (structural and functional) signs of aging and to explore/identify other possible biological measures of aging.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

The claim is marked as 'indirect' but is presented as a statement of study aims. However, the citation trace for reference [30] (NCT04994561) indicates that the study status is 'withdrawn' and 'has_results=False'. Because the study was withdrawn and has no published results, it cannot provide evidence for the safety or efficacy of the drugs mentioned. Furthermore, the claim is essentially a repetition of the study's objective rather than a finding, and since there are no results, there is no evidence to support any assertion of efficacy or safety.

**Flagged claims:** C070

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 1)

The submission claims to assess the safety and efficacy of drugs on signs of aging based on a single reference (NCT04994561). However, the citation trace reveals that this clinical trial was withdrawn and has produced no results. Framing the 'aims' of a failed or withdrawn study as a basis for a claim about efficacy is a fundamental failure of evidence calibration. There is no data to support any conclusion regarding safety or efficacy.

**Flagged claims:** C070

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 1)

Both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic correctly identified a critical failure in the evidence chain. The claim presents the objectives of a clinical trial (NCT04994561) as a basis for assessing safety and efficacy, yet the citation trace confirms the study was withdrawn and produced no results. A statement of intent from a withdrawn study cannot serve as evidence for efficacy or safety. There is no dissent to weigh in this case; the lack of empirical data renders the claim baseless.

**Flagged claims:** C070

## References

[30] VIAging Deceleration Trial Using Metformin, Dasatinib, Rapamycin and Nutritional Supplements (2023). https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04994561
