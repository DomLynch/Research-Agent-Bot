# Submission rejected

## DRAFT REJECTED

**SPAR verdict:** `reject_critical` (3-0 unanimous reject).

## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> the practical implementation of lifestyle modification or the use of metformin for treating prediabetes is inadequate and complicated by a lack of agreement on how to define the condition.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission fails the evidence audit due to a critical lack of source data. While the citation traces indicate a 'PASS' for role matching, there are no provided source abstracts or published results to verify the actual content of reference [18]. Consequently, the numeric and qualitative assertions in C042 (cost-effectiveness, prevention of diabetes, improvement of CV risk) and C043 (inadequate implementation, lack of definition agreement) cannot be traced to any evidence. Claims marked as 'mechanistic' without accompanying published results or verifiable text are unsupported.

**Flagged claims:** C042, C043

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission suffers from significant mechanism inflation and a lack of scoping. Claim C042 makes broad assertions regarding cost-effectiveness and cardiovascular risk improvement based on a single reference, without specifying the population, duration of follow-up, or the specific 'pharmacologic interventions' used. Framing these as established 'demonstrations' without caveats regarding the heterogeneity of RCT results is over-claiming.

Furthermore, Claim C043 makes a sweeping sociological and clinical assertion that implementation is 'inadequate' and 'complicated' by definitional disagreements. This is a qualitative judgment presented as a mechanistic claim. There is no evidence provided to quantify this 'inadequacy' or to demonstrate that the lack of a universal definition is the primary driver of implementation failure rather than patient adherence, cost, or provider preference.

**Flagged claims:** C042, C043

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission is fundamentally flawed due to a complete absence of verifiable source text for reference [18], rendering the claims unfalsifiable and unsupported. Even if the source were present, the Domain Skeptic correctly identifies that C042 over-generalizes the outcomes of RCTs without necessary nuance, and C043 attempts to present a qualitative, sociological observation as a 'mechanistic' claim, which is a category error in research framing.

There is no dissent to weigh in this case; both prior reviewers identified critical failures in evidence transparency and claim scoping. The lack of specificity regarding the 'pharmacologic interventions' and the failure to quantify the 'inadequacy' of implementation make these claims too vague for academic acceptance.

**Flagged claims:** C042, C043
