## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> Both metabolic and nonmetabolic pathways were significantly influenced, including pyruvate metabolism and DNA repair in muscle and PPAR and SREBP signaling, mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation, and collagen trimerization in adipose.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 2)

The submission fails the evidence audit because it lacks the necessary source abstracts to verify the numeric values and specific pathway claims. While the role matches passed, there is no provided evidence to trace the specific numbers in C030 (647 genes, 146 genes) or the specific biological pathways listed in C031 (pyruvate metabolism, DNA repair, PPAR, SREBP, etc.). Without the source text for reference [14], these claims are unsupported numeric and qualitative assertions.

**Flagged claims:** C029, C030, C031

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 4)

The submission suffers from significant mechanism inflation and a lack of necessary caveats. Claim C031 frames the 'influence' of metabolic and nonmetabolic pathways as a direct result of metformin, but it relies on differential gene expression (DGE) data. DGE is a proxy for potential activity, not a direct measurement of pathway flux or functional outcome. To claim that DNA repair or collagen trimerization were 'significantly influenced' based solely on transcriptomics without functional validation is an over-claim.

Furthermore, the submission fails to provide the context of the study population or sample size. Given that these are highly specific tissue-level changes (muscle and adipose), the lack of information regarding the cohort's characteristics (e.g., BMI, baseline insulin resistance, or duration of therapy) makes it impossible to determine if these results are generalizable or specific to a narrow sub-population. The leap from 'differentially expressed genes' to 'influenced pathways' is too broad for a high-confidence direct claim.

**Flagged claims:** C031

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission is fundamentally flawed due to a total absence of source text for reference [14], rendering the specific numeric claims in C030 and the detailed pathway assertions in C031 unverifiable. In a research review context, high-confidence claims involving specific gene counts and biological pathways cannot be accepted on faith without the accompanying evidence for audit.

Furthermore, I find the Domain Skeptic's critique of C031 particularly compelling. The transition from differential gene expression (DGE) to the claim that pathways were 'significantly influenced' is a common but significant over-reach in transcriptomic reporting. Without functional validation or flux analysis, claiming a direct influence on DNA repair or collagen trimerization based solely on mRNA levels is an over-interpretation of the data. Combined with the lack of cohort context, the submission fails to meet the standards for a high-confidence direct claim.

**Flagged claims:** C029, C030, C031
