### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 2)

The submission suffers from extreme over-claiming and a lack of calibration. Claim C075 frames the objective as assessing the 'efficacy' of metformin in 'mitigating the aging process' in humans. Aging is a complex, systemic biological phenomenon; claiming to mitigate the process itself—rather than specific age-related comorbidities or biomarkers—is a massive leap in scope that is rarely supported by clinical data. 

Furthermore, the claim attempts to establish 'innovative frameworks' for pharmaceutical interventions in aging based on a single reference. This is a classic case of mechanism inflation, where the goal of a study is conflated with a proven outcome. The scope is far too broad for the evidence provided, and the ambition of the thesis exceeds the scientific reality of current metformin research in human senescence.

**Flagged claims:** C075

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 1)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission fails on two fundamental levels: empirical integrity and scientific plausibility. The Evidence Auditor identified a critical failure in the citation trace, where the primary supporting reference [38] relies on a non-existent clinical trial identifier (NCT06459310). This suggests a fabrication of evidence, which is an automatic disqualifier for research validity.

Furthermore, I concur with the Domain Skeptic regarding the conceptual overreach of the thesis. Claiming to 'mitigate the aging process' in humans is a systemic assertion that exceeds the current clinical understanding of metformin, which typically targets specific metabolic markers or comorbidities rather than senescence itself. The combination of a fabricated reference and an inflated scientific scope makes this submission entirely untenable.

**Flagged claims:** C075
