## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

The submission fails the evidence audit due to a failed citation trace. The supporting reference [30] for claim C070 relies on a clinical trial identifier (NCT04994561) that was not found in the registry, indicating the source is either fabricated or stale. Per the auditor guidelines, any failed trace is grounds for immediate rejection.

**Flagged claims:** C070

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 2)

The submission fails on a fundamental level of scoping and evidence. The claim [C070] is framed as a statement of a study's aims, yet it provides no actual results, data, or conclusions regarding the safety or efficacy of metformin (or any other drug) on aging. It is essentially a proposal or a description of a trial's intent, not a scientific finding. 

Furthermore, the claim is categorized as 'indirect' and 'low' strength, yet it attempts to establish a framework for assessing 'clinical signs of aging,' which is a highly complex and contested domain. There is a massive leap from 'aiming to assess' to providing any actionable evidence for the thesis. The submission offers no data to support the efficacy of the intervention, making the claim an empty shell of an objective rather than a supported scientific conclusion.

**Flagged claims:** C070

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 1)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission fails on two critical fronts: evidentiary integrity and scientific substance. First, the citation trace failed because the NCT identifier provided in reference [30] is non-existent or fabricated, which is a fatal flaw in a research review. Second, as the Domain Skeptic noted, the claim is merely a statement of intent (a study aim) rather than a report of findings or data. A statement of what a study 'aims to assess' does not constitute evidence of safety or efficacy. Given the fabrication of the source and the lack of actual results, there is no basis for acceptance.

**Flagged claims:** C070
