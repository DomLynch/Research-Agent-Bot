## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

Both claims C073 and C074 rely on a single supporting reference [34]. The citation trace for this reference failed the nct_exists check, indicating that the NCT identifier 'NCT03107884' is fabricated or does not exist in the registry. Because the evidence source is invalid, the claims are unsupported.

**Flagged claims:** C073, C074

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission makes a strong clinical claim in C073 regarding the attenuation of muscle loss in adults with insulin resistance. However, this is a highly contentious area of pharmacology; metformin is generally known to improve insulin sensitivity, but its effect on muscle mass is often neutral or even potentially negative (interference with hypertrophy) depending on the population and exercise context. Framing it as a proven 'attenuation of muscle loss' without specifying the population, dosage, or the presence of concurrent exercise is a significant over-claim.

Furthermore, the submission relies entirely on a single reference [34] which, according to the trace, points to a non-existent or fabricated NCT registry number. Relying on a single, potentially fraudulent source to support a claim that contradicts or complicates established physiological understanding of metformin's effect on skeletal muscle is unacceptable.

**Flagged claims:** C073

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 1)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission fails on two critical levels: evidentiary integrity and scientific accuracy. First, the sole supporting reference [34] relies on a fabricated or non-existent NCT registry number, rendering the claims entirely unsupported by the provided evidence. Second, as noted by the Domain Skeptic, the claim that metformin 'attenuates muscle loss' is clinically contentious and potentially contradictory to established pharmacological knowledge regarding metformin's interference with muscle hypertrophy. The combination of a fraudulent citation and an over-generalized clinical claim makes this submission ineligible for acceptance.

**Flagged claims:** C073, C074
