# CT scan showed that increases in thigh muscle area (p = .005) and density (p = .020) were greater in placebo versus metformin

**SPAR verdict:** `accept_caveated` (2-1 accept).

**Dissent — `domain_skeptic`:** The submission suffers from significant mechanism inflation and over-claiming. While C031-C033 report specific statistical findings from a single study (NCT02308228), C035 transforms these specific observations into a broad, definitive conclusion that metformin 'negatively impacts the hypertrophic response' in healthy older individuals. This is an over-generalization based on a single trial, failing to account for potential variability in dosage, duration, or the specific population of 'healthy older individuals' beyond the study's specific cohort.

Furthermore, C034 frames a 'trend' in mTORC1 signaling as a direct effect. In clinical research, a 'trend' is statistically non-significant; presenting it alongside a direct claim of AMPK increase without the caveat of non-significance for mTORC1 is misleading. The jump from molecular signaling trends and specific muscle area measurements to a general conclusion about hypertrophic response is an extrapolation that exceeds the strength of the provided evidence.

## Thesis

CT scan showed that increases in thigh muscle area (p = .005) and density (p = .020) were greater in placebo versus metformin [15].

## Findings

placebo gained more lean body mass (p = .003) and thigh muscle mass (p < .001) than metformin [15].
placebo had decreased type I fiber percentage while metformin did not (p = .007) [15].
Metformin led to an increase in AMPK signaling, and a trend for blunted increases in mTORC1 signaling in response to PRT [15].
metformin negatively impacts the hypertrophic response to resistance training in healthy older individuals [15].
