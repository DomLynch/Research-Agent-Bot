### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission suffers from severe mechanism inflation and over-claiming. The thesis (C005) presents broad clinical-sounding outcomes—such as improving arterial stiffness and alleviating structural changes in aged arteries—but the supporting evidence is derived from a single source (Ref 3) focusing on Ang II-induced senescence in VSMCs. This is a classic case of extrapolating preclinical, cell-culture, or animal-model mechanistic data to general physiological outcomes without specifying the model or the limitations of the findings.

**Flagged claims:** C005, C008

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission fails on two critical levels: evidentiary transparency and scientific rigor. First, as noted by the Auditor, the absence of source text for Reference [3] makes the specific mechanistic claims regarding autophagic flux and autolysosome formation (C006, C007, C008) unverifiable. Second, I agree with the Skeptic that the thesis (C005) commits a significant leap in logic by presenting broad physiological improvements in 'aged arteries' based on a single study that appears to focus on Ang II-induced senescence in VSMCs. This extrapolation from a specific stimulus-induced model to general aging is an over-claim that undermines the validity of the research.

**Flagged claims:** C004, C005, C006, C007, C008
