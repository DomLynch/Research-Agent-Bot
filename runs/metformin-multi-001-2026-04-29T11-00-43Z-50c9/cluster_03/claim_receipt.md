## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> within diabetics, the strong boosting effect of the high-dose vaccine was significantly dampened for metformin users (n = 64) as compared to non-users (n = 30), but still remained slightly higher than standard-dose responses.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 3)

Claim C056 contains specific numeric values (n = 64 and n = 30) that are not verified by the provided citation traces. While the p-values for C057 and C058 were successfully traced to the source abstract, there is no trace confirming the existence of these sample sizes in the source text. As an Evidence Auditor, I must reject any submission where numeric values in claims are not explicitly supported by the evidence traces.

**Flagged claims:** C056

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 4)

The submission presents a highly specific clinical observation based on an extremely small sample size (n=94 total diabetics, with only 30 non-users) as a generalizable claim. The disparity in group sizes (64 vs 30) introduces significant risk of selection bias and reduces the statistical power to ensure these findings aren't driven by a few outliers within the smaller cohort. 

Furthermore, the claims fail to account for critical confounders inherent to metformin users. Patients on metformin typically have different glycemic control levels, comorbidities, and durations of diabetes compared to non-users. Attributing the 'dampening effect' solely to metformin without controlling for these variables is a mechanistic leap that the provided evidence does not support. The conclusion is over-scoped by presenting a correlation as a direct effect of the drug.

**Flagged claims:** C056, C057, C058

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 3)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The Evidence Auditor correctly identifies a critical failure in the audit trail: the specific sample sizes (n=64, n=30) cited in C056 were not verified by the citation traces. In a research review, the presence of unverified numeric data in a primary claim is a disqualifying error. 

Furthermore, I find the Domain Skeptic's concerns regarding the clinical validity of the claim compelling. The small, unbalanced sample size and the lack of mentioned controls for glycemic status or comorbidities make the attribution of the 'dampening effect' to metformin alone scientifically tenuous. While the p-values in C057 and C058 were traced, they do not rescue the thesis because they rely on the flawed population parameters established in C056. The submission presents a specific correlation as a generalizable effect without sufficient evidence or methodological rigor.

**Flagged claims:** C056, C057, C058
