**SPAR verdict:** `reject_critical` (3-0 unanimous reject).

## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> Fasting or treatment with metformin can reverse these changes and restore the regenerative capacity of aged OPCs, improving remyelination in aged animals following focal demyelination.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 2)

The submission fails the evidence audit due to a complete lack of source abstracts. While the citation traces indicate a 'PASS' for role and alias matching, there is no provided text from reference [4] to verify the actual claims. As an Evidence Auditor, I cannot confirm that the specific assertions regarding 'regenerative capacity of aged OPCs' or 'responsiveness to pro-differentiation signals' are supported by the evidence when the evidence itself is missing from the input. Furthermore, the trace results provided are tautological (matching the claim to itself) rather than verifying the claim against a source text.

**Flagged claims:** C009, C010

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission suffers from severe mechanism inflation and over-claiming. Claim C009 asserts that metformin can 'reverse' aging changes and 'restore' regenerative capacity to improve remyelination in aged animals. This is a high-level functional claim based on a single reference, likely from a preclinical animal model, yet it is framed as a definitive restorative capability without specifying the species or the limitations of the focal demyelination model. 

Furthermore, C010 extrapolates a 'synergistic effect' between rejuvenation and pro-differentiation therapies. This is a speculative leap; observing that a cell 'regains responsiveness' is not evidence of synergy with other therapies unless those therapies were tested in combination. The claims are over-scoped and fail to provide the necessary caveats regarding the translation from preclinical OPC (Oligodendrocyte Precursor Cell) behavior to actual clinical outcomes in aged populations.

**Flagged claims:** C009, C010

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission is fundamentally flawed due to a total absence of source text for reference [4], rendering the claims unverifiable. The citation traces provided are merely structural checks (confirming the claim matches the claim) rather than evidentiary validations against a source. 

Beyond the lack of evidence, the Domain Skeptic correctly identifies significant over-claiming. The use of definitive terms like 'reverse' and 'restore' in C009, and the speculative leap to 'synergistic effects' in C010, represents a level of mechanism inflation that is unacceptable without rigorous, multi-source evidence and explicit caveats regarding the animal models used. The lack of transparency regarding the source material combined with the aggressive framing of the results necessitates a critical rejection.

**Flagged claims:** C009, C010
