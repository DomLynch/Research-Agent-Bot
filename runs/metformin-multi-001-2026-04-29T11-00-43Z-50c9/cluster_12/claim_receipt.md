# Submission rejected

## DRAFT REJECTED

**SPAR verdict:** `reject_critical` (3-0 unanimous reject).

## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> This study aims to assess the safety and efficacy of study drugs and supplements on clinical (structural and functional) signs of aging and to explore/identify other possible biological measures of aging.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

The claim is marked as 'indirect' but is presented as a statement of the study's aims. However, the citation trace for NCT04994561 reveals that the study status is 'withdrawn' and 'has_results=False'. Because the study was withdrawn and has no published results, it cannot provide evidence for the safety or efficacy of the drugs mentioned, nor can it support a claim regarding the assessment of biological measures of aging in a meaningful research context. Furthermore, the claim is a verbatim repetition of the thesis, but it lacks a published_results citation to support any actual findings, rendering the evidence void.

**Flagged claims:** C069

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 1)

The submission attempts to frame the goals of a specific study (NCT04994561) as a valid claim of efficacy or safety assessment. However, the citation trace reveals that the study in question was withdrawn and has produced no results. Claiming that a study 'aims to assess' something is a statement of intent, not a statement of evidence, and in this case, the intent was never realized due to the study's withdrawal.

Furthermore, the claim is functionally empty; it describes a study's objective rather than providing a scientific conclusion about metformin. Using a withdrawn trial to support a thesis on the safety and efficacy of aging interventions is a fundamental failure of evidence calibration.

**Flagged claims:** C069

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 1)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission fails on two fundamental levels: first, the claim is a statement of intent (an 'aim') rather than a statement of finding, which provides no scientific value to the thesis. Second, the citation provided (NCT04994561) refers to a study that was withdrawn and produced no results. A withdrawn trial cannot serve as evidence for safety or efficacy, nor can it support a research claim in any meaningful capacity. There is no dissent to weigh in this case, as the evidence is non-existent.

**Flagged claims:** C069
