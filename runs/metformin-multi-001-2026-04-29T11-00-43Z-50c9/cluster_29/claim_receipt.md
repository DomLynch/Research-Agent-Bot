## DRAFT REJECTED

**SPAR verdict:** `reject_critical` (3-0 unanimous reject).

## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> Notably, we showed that long-term metformin administration at clinically relevant doses effectively delays cochlear aging in primates.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

The submission fails the evidence audit because there is no source abstract provided for reference [9]. As the Evidence Auditor, I cannot verify if the claim regarding metformin delaying cochlear aging in primates is supported by the evidence, nor can I verify the 'clinically relevant doses' mentioned in the claim. A claim marked as 'mechanistic' (direct) requires a published_results citation that can be traced to the text; without the source text, the trace is incomplete and the numeric/qualitative assertions are unsupported.

**Flagged claims:** C019

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission makes a sweeping claim about delaying 'cochlear aging' in primates based on a single reference. In pharmacological research, 'delaying aging' is a high-bar conclusion that requires longitudinal data across multiple cohorts and a rigorous definition of aging biomarkers. The claim fails to specify which primate species was used, the duration of 'long-term' administration, or the specific functional outcomes (e.g., ABR thresholds, hair cell density) that justify the term 'effectively delays.'

Furthermore, there is a significant risk of mechanism inflation here. Results in non-human primates, while more relevant than rodents, cannot be framed as a definitive 'showing' of aging delay without extensive caveats regarding the translation to human cochlear physiology and the potential for off-target effects. The claim is over-scoped and lacks the necessary nuance regarding the specific parameters of the 'delay' observed.

**Flagged claims:** C019

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 1)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission suffers from a critical failure of transparency: the absence of the source text for reference [9] makes the claim unverifiable. In a research review panel, a claim cannot be accepted based on a citation trace that lacks the underlying evidence for audit. Furthermore, I agree with the Domain Skeptic that the claim is over-scoped; asserting that a drug 'effectively delays cochlear aging' without specifying the primate species, the duration of the study, or the specific biomarkers measured is scientifically imprecise and constitutes mechanism inflation. The lack of supporting data combined with the sweeping nature of the conclusion necessitates a rejection.

**Flagged claims:** C019
