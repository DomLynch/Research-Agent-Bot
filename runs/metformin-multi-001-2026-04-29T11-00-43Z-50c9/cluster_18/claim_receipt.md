# Submission rejected

## DRAFT REJECTED

**SPAR verdict:** `reject_critical` (3-0 unanimous reject).

## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> Collagen content and myofiber size corresponded to reduced whole muscle cellular senescence and SASP markers.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

The submission contains a critical failure in the citation trace for claim C013. The alias match for 'Collagen' resolved to 'CAPTOPRIL', which is a completely different pharmacological agent and unrelated to the structural protein collagen. This indicates a fundamental failure in the evidence trace, as the claim regarding collagen content and myofiber size is being supported by data pertaining to a different drug.

**Flagged claims:** C013

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 2)

The submission suffers from a critical domain error and a fundamental failure in evidence mapping. The citation trace reveals that 'Collagen' in claim C013 was resolved to 'CAPTOPRIL'. Captopril is an ACE inhibitor, not metformin. This is a severe instance of off-topic drift where evidence for an entirely different pharmacological agent is being used to support claims about metformin's effect on muscle senescence and collagen content.

Furthermore, the claims regarding myofiber atrophy and FAP senescence (C012, C014) are presented as mechanistic certainties, yet the evidence is flagged as 'low' confidence. The leap from these markers to a general conclusion about reduced cellular senescence in whole muscle is an over-extrapolation of preclinical or narrow mechanistic markers to a broader physiological outcome without sufficient caveats regarding the model used (e.g., whether this was in vitro, rodent, or human).

**Flagged claims:** C012, C013, C014

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 1)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission contains a catastrophic failure in the citation trace for C013, where 'Collagen' was erroneously resolved to 'CAPTOPRIL'. This is not a minor alias error but a fundamental hallucination in the evidence mapping process, substituting an ACE inhibitor for a structural protein/metformin context. This invalidates the primary thesis [C013].

Additionally, I concur with the Domain Skeptic regarding the fragility of claims C012 and C014. The submission attempts to establish mechanistic certainty while simultaneously labeling the evidence as 'low' confidence, creating a logical contradiction. The leap from specific FAP markers to a generalized reduction in whole-muscle senescence is an over-extrapolation that lacks the necessary nuance or model-specific caveats. Given the combination of a critical mapping error and poor evidentiary rigor, the submission is entirely unsupported.

**Flagged claims:** C012, C013, C014
