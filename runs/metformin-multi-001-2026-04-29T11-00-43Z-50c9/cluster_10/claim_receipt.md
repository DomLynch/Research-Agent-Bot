# Submission rejected

## DRAFT REJECTED

**SPAR verdict:** `reject_critical` (3-0 unanimous reject).

## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> Metformin treatment has been shown to improve insulin sensitivity and attenuate muscle loss in insulin resistance adults though the mechanisms are not fully known.

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 1)

Claim C072 is marked as 'direct' and asserts that metformin 'has been shown to improve' specific outcomes, yet the supporting citation (Ref 34, NCT03107884) is a clinical trial that is 'active_not_recruiting' and has 'has_results=False'. A study without published results cannot provide the evidence required for a direct claim of efficacy. Furthermore, the evidence auditor requires a published_results citation for claims marked as direct; the provided trace confirms no such results exist for this NCT ID.

**Flagged claims:** C072

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 2)

The submission suffers from severe over-claiming and a fundamental failure to account for the status of the supporting evidence. Claim C072 asserts that metformin 'has been shown' to improve insulin sensitivity and attenuate muscle loss, yet the only supporting reference provided (NCT03107884) is a clinical trial that is listed as 'active, not recruiting' with no results reported. It is logically impossible to claim a result 'has been shown' based on a trial that has not yet published its findings.

Furthermore, the claim regarding the attenuation of muscle loss is highly contentious in the domain of metabolic research; metformin is often scrutinized for potentially inhibiting hypertrophy or interfering with muscle protein synthesis (the 'metformin-exercise paradox'). Presenting a definitive positive outcome on muscle mass without citing peer-reviewed, completed results—and relying instead on an incomplete trial—is a critical failure in evidence calibration.

**Flagged claims:** C072

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 1)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission contains a critical failure in evidence calibration: Claim C072 asserts that a specific effect 'has been shown,' yet the only supporting evidence provided is a clinical trial (NCT03107884) that is still active and has not published results. It is logically impossible to substantiate a claim of proven efficacy using a study that has not yet yielded data.

Furthermore, I find the Domain Skeptic's point regarding the 'metformin-exercise paradox' highly relevant. Given that the effect of metformin on muscle mass is a subject of significant academic debate, the lack of peer-reviewed, completed results makes the claim not only unsupported but potentially misleading. There is no dissenting view to weigh in this instance; the evidence gap is absolute.

**Flagged claims:** C072
