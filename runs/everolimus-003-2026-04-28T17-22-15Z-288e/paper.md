# We found a statistically significant reduction in the proportion of patients who had one or more laboratory-confirmed RTIs in the RTB101 10 mg once daily treatment group (34 [19%] of 176) compared with the pooled placebo group (50 [28%] of 180; odds ratio [OR] 0·601 [90% CI 0·391-0·922]; p=0·02)

**SPAR verdict:** `accept_caveated` (2-1 accept).

**Dissent — `domain_skeptic`:** The submission suffers from a critical internal contradiction and a failure to scope the findings. Claim C017 explicitly states that the efficacy of RTB101 10 mg in combination with everolimus 'did not meet statistical significance,' yet Claim C018 immediately asserts a 'statistically significant reduction' in laboratory-confirmed RTIs for the exact same treatment group. This creates a logical paradox within the thesis that is not resolved by any provided caveat.

Furthermore, C018 presents a result based on a 'pooled placebo group.' Pooling data from different arms or studies without explicit justification regarding the homogeneity of those groups introduces significant confounding risks and can artificially inflate statistical significance. The submission fails to address whether this pooling was pre-specified or a post-hoc analysis to find a significant p-value, which is a major red flag for over-claiming.

## Thesis

we found a statistically significant reduction in the proportion of patients who had one or more laboratory-confirmed RTIs in the RTB101 10 mg once daily treatment group (34 [19%] of 176) compared with the pooled placebo group (50 [28%] of 180; odds ratio [OR] 0·601 [90% CI 0·391-0·922]; p=0·02) [7].

## Findings

efficacy of RTB101 10 mg in combination with everolimus 0·1 mg once daily compared with placebo did not meet statistical significance [7].
