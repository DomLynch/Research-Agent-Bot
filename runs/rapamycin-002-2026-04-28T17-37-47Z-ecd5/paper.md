# Lean tissue mass (ηp2 = 0.202, p = 0.013) and self-reported pain (ηp2 = 0.168, p = 0.015) improved significantly for women using 10 mg rapamycin

**SPAR verdict:** `accept_caveated` (2-1 accept).

**Dissent — `domain_skeptic`:** The submission presents results from a single study (NCT04488601) as high-confidence direct claims without providing the necessary context regarding sample size, study duration, or the specific population (e.g., postmenopausal women). Reporting p-values and partial eta squared (ηp2) without the absolute change in lean tissue mass or the baseline characteristics of the participants is a form of mechanism inflation; statistical significance does not automatically equate to clinical significance, especially in small-cohort pilot studies.

Furthermore, the claim [C013] links rapamycin use to an improvement in 'self-reported pain' and 'lean tissue mass' without mentioning potential confounders such as concurrent exercise or dietary interventions, which are common in longevity-focused trials. The leap from a single trial's p-values to a high-confidence 'direct' claim is over-scoped and fails to account for the high variability in rapamycin's effects on muscle mass (which can be inhibitory in some contexts).

## Thesis

Lean tissue mass (ηp2 = 0.202, p = 0.013) and self-reported pain (ηp2 = 0.168, p = 0.015) improved significantly for women using 10 mg rapamycin [3].

## Findings

Visceral adiposity did not change significantly (ηp2 = 0.001, p = 0.942) [3].
Self-reported emotional well-being (ηp2 = 0.108, p = 0.023) and general health (ηp2 = 0.166, p = 0.004) also improved for those using 5 mg rapamycin [3].
