# Lean tissue mass (ηp2 = 0.202, p = 0.013) and self-reported pain (ηp2 = 0.168, p = 0.015) improved significantly for women using 10 mg rapamycin

**SPAR verdict:** `accept_caveated` (2-1 accept).

**Dissent — `domain_skeptic`:** The submission presents results from a single study (NCT04488601) as definitive improvements without providing the necessary context regarding sample size, study duration, or control group comparisons. Specifically, claiming that rapamycin 'improved' lean tissue mass and pain in women based on p-values and partial eta squared (ηp2) from a single trial is an over-extension of the evidence. 

Furthermore, the submission fails to mention critical confounders common in rapamycin trials, such as concurrent exercise or dietary interventions, which could independently drive changes in lean tissue mass and self-reported pain. The reliance on 'self-reported' metrics for pain and emotional well-being without acknowledging the high risk of placebo effect in small-scale longevity or wellness trials constitutes a significant lack of necessary caveats.

## Thesis

Lean tissue mass (ηp2 = 0.202, p = 0.013) and self-reported pain (ηp2 = 0.168, p = 0.015) improved significantly for women using 10 mg rapamycin [3].

## Findings

Visceral adiposity did not change significantly (ηp2 = 0.001, p = 0.942) [3].
Self-reported pain (ηp2 = 0.168, p = 0.015) improved significantly for women using 10 mg rapamycin [3].
Self-reported emotional well-being (ηp2 = 0.108, p = 0.023) and general health (ηp2 = 0.166, p = 0.004) also improved for those using 5 mg rapamycin [3].
