## Proposed thesis (rejected)

> we found a statistically significant reduction in the proportion of patients who had one or more laboratory-confirmed RTIs in the RTB101 10 mg once daily treatment group (34 [19%] of 176) compared with the pooled placebo group (50 [28%] of 180; odds ratio [OR] 0·601 [90% CI 0·391-0·922]; p=0·02)

## Panel reviews

### `evidence_auditor` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission contains a fundamental internal contradiction between Claim C019 and Claim C020, both of which cite the same source [7]. C019 asserts that the efficacy of RTB101 10 mg 'did not meet statistical significance,' while C020 explicitly claims a 'statistically significant reduction' with a provided p-value of 0.02. 

Furthermore, while the trace for C020 confirms the presence of percentages (19%, 28%, 90%), it fails to verify the critical numeric values and the p-value (0.02) against the source abstract. Because the claims are mutually exclusive regarding statistical significance and the specific numeric outcomes of the trial are not fully traced/verified, the evidence does not consistently support the assertions.

**Flagged claims:** C019, C020

### `domain_skeptic` — `reject` (score 3)

The submission contains a fundamental internal contradiction between C019 and C020 that is not reconciled. C019 explicitly states that the efficacy of RTB101 10 mg did not meet statistical significance, yet C020 claims a 'statistically significant reduction' in laboratory-confirmed RTIs for the exact same treatment group (RTB101 10 mg) using the same reference [7]. This suggests a failure to distinguish between primary endpoints and secondary/exploratory endpoints, or a failure to account for multiplicity adjustments.

Furthermore, C020 relies on a 'pooled placebo group.' Pooling placebo arms across different cohorts or trials without rigorous justification often introduces significant confounders and can artificially inflate statistical significance. By presenting the result as a direct high-confidence claim without mentioning that the placebo group was pooled or clarifying why the primary endpoint failed (C019) while this specific metric succeeded (C020), the submission over-scopes the clinical utility of the drug.

**Flagged claims:** C019, C020

### `final_judge` — `reject` (score 2)

I align with both the Evidence Auditor and the Domain Skeptic. The submission presents a critical internal contradiction: C019 states the treatment did not meet statistical significance, while C020 claims a statistically significant reduction for the same treatment group using the same reference. This is a fundamental failure in reporting clinical trial data, as it fails to distinguish between the primary endpoint (which likely failed, as per C019) and a secondary or pooled analysis (C020).

I place significant weight on the Domain Skeptic's observation regarding the 'pooled placebo group.' Reporting a statistically significant result from a pooled analysis while the primary efficacy endpoint failed is a common form of data dredging or selective reporting if not explicitly contextualized. Because the submission presents these as two high-confidence direct claims without explaining the relationship between the failure of the primary endpoint and the success of the pooled secondary analysis, the submission is misleading and scientifically unsound.

**Flagged claims:** C019, C020
