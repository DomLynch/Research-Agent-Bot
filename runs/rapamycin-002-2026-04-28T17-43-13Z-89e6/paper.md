# Lean tissue mass (ηp2 = 0.202, p = 0.013) and self-reported pain (ηp2 = 0.168, p = 0.015) improved significantly for women using 10 mg rapamycin

**SPAR verdict:** `accept_caveated` (2-1 accept).

**Dissent — `domain_skeptic`:** The submission presents statistical results from a single study (NCT04488601) as high-confidence direct claims without providing the necessary context regarding sample size or study design. In clinical research, reporting p-values and partial eta squared (ηp2) without the total N or the confidence intervals is a major red flag for over-claiming. Small sample sizes can produce 'significant' p-values that are clinically irrelevant or non-reproducible.

Furthermore, the claims regarding 'self-reported pain' and 'emotional well-being' are highly susceptible to placebo effects, especially in an open-label or small-scale trial. Framing these subjective improvements as 'direct' high-confidence outcomes of rapamycin administration—without mentioning the potential for confounders or the specific population characteristics of the trial—is an over-extension of the evidence. The submission treats a single trial's data as an established fact rather than a preliminary finding.

## Thesis

Lean tissue mass (ηp2 = 0.202, p = 0.013) and self-reported pain (ηp2 = 0.168, p = 0.015) improved significantly for women using 10 mg rapamycin [3].

## Findings

Visceral adiposity did not change significantly (ηp2 = 0.001, p = 0.942) [3].
Self-reported emotional well-being (ηp2 = 0.108, p = 0.023) and general health (ηp2 = 0.166, p = 0.004) also improved for those using 5 mg rapamycin [3].
