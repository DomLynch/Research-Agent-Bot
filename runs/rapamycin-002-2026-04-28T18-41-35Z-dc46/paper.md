# Lean tissue mass (ηp2 = 0.202, p = 0.013) and self-reported pain (ηp2 = 0.168, p = 0.015) improved significantly for women using 10 mg rapamycin

**SPAR verdict:** `accept_caveated` (2-1 accept).

**Dissent — `domain_skeptic`:** The submission presents results from a single study (NCT04488601) as definitive improvements without providing the necessary context regarding sample size, study design, or the nature of the 'improvements.' Specifically, claiming that rapamycin 'improved' lean tissue mass and self-reported pain in women based on a single trial—likely a small-scale pilot given the specific effect sizes and p-values—is a significant over-reach. 

Furthermore, the submission fails to mention critical confounders. Rapamycin is an mTOR inhibitor; its known pharmacological profile typically involves the inhibition of muscle protein synthesis, making a claim of 'improved lean tissue mass' highly counter-intuitive and potentially an artifact of a small sample size or specific study population (e.g., those with specific comorbidities). Without a discussion of the baseline characteristics or the duration of the intervention, these claims are mechanically suspect and over-scoped.

## Thesis

Lean tissue mass (ηp2 = 0.202, p = 0.013) and self-reported pain (ηp2 = 0.168, p = 0.015) improved significantly for women using 10 mg rapamycin [3].

## Findings

Visceral adiposity did not change significantly (ηp2 = 0.001, p = 0.942) [3].
Self-reported pain (ηp2 = 0.168, p = 0.015) improved significantly for women using 10 mg rapamycin [3].
Self-reported emotional well-being (ηp2 = 0.108, p = 0.023) and general health (ηp2 = 0.166, p = 0.004) also improved for those using 5 mg rapamycin [3].
