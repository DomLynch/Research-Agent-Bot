# Both metabolic and nonmetabolic pathways were significantly influenced, including pyruvate metabolism and DNA repair in muscle and PPAR and SREBP signaling, mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation, and collagen trimerization in adipose

**SPAR verdict:** `accept_caveated` (2-1 accept).

**Dissent — `domain_skeptic`:** The submission presents findings from a study with a critically small sample size (n=14) as high-confidence direct evidence. In transcriptomic research, a sample size of 14 is highly susceptible to noise and individual variability, making the claim of 'significantly influenced' pathways (C010) over-scoped. The submission fails to mention this limitation, framing the results as definitive rather than exploratory.

Furthermore, the claims regarding differentially expressed genes (C009) and pathway influence (C010) are based on a short-term (6-week) intervention. Extrapolating these transient transcriptomic shifts to broader 'metabolic and nonmetabolic pathways' without qualifying the duration or the specific population (older adults) constitutes mechanism inflation. The evidence supports a preliminary observation in a small cohort, not a generalized high-confidence effect.

## Thesis

Both metabolic and nonmetabolic pathways were significantly influenced, including pyruvate metabolism and DNA repair in muscle and PPAR and SREBP signaling, mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation, and collagen trimerization in adipose [3].

## Findings

metformin therapy lowered 2-hour glucose, insulin AUC, and insulin secretion compared to placebo [3].
Using FDR<0.05, 647 genes were differentially expressed in muscle and 146 genes were differentially expressed in adipose tissue [3].
