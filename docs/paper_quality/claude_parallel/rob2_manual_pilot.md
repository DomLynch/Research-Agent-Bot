# Risk of Bias Manual Pilot — Rapamycin Human RCTs

**Status:** Provisional manual pilot. Reasoning from the AAA4 paper's synthesis description, public abstracts, and field knowledge — *not* from full source-text reading of each trial's protocol and reporting documents. Use as a template for the source-text RoB 2 pass, not as a finished assessment.
**Generated:** 2026-05-09
**Tool:** Cochrane RoB 2 (RCTs); ROBINS-I noted for the one observational cohort; SYRCLE noted for animal evidence.
**Purpose:** Demonstrate what a source-text RoB 2 pass would look like once activated; provide the `domain × study` matrix that the bot's `scripts/risk_of_bias.py` scaffold needs to populate; surface the source-text data fields the extraction pipeline must capture per study before automation.

---

## Why this pilot matters

The AAA4 paper currently includes a "Table 4: Per-Domain Risk of Bias + Synthesis Weight" generated by tier-and-directness rollup, *not* by source-text Cochrane RoB 2 / ROBINS-I assessment. The table itself flags this honestly: "Per-domain grades + the named RoB tool are derived from each study's evidence tier (A1/A2/B1/B2/C1/C2) — they capture design-level limitations, NOT a per-paper Cochrane RoB-2 / ROBINS-I assessment from the source text."

This is a desk-rejection-grade limitation for a senior-PhD review. RoB 2 must be applied from source text. This pilot demonstrates the pass and identifies the missing data fields.

---

## Cochrane RoB 2 framework (RCT studies)

Cochrane RoB 2 evaluates five domains, each with three to seven signaling questions, producing a domain-level judgment and an overall judgment.

| Domain | What it assesses |
|---|---|
| 1. Randomization process | Sequence generation, allocation concealment, baseline balance |
| 2. Deviations from intended interventions | Blinding, protocol adherence, ITT vs PP analysis |
| 3. Missing outcome data | Attrition, missing-data mechanism, sensitivity analysis |
| 4. Measurement of the outcome | Outcome assessor blinding, measurement validity, equal application |
| 5. Selection of the reported result | Pre-registration, deviations from protocol, multiple analyses |

**Judgment values:** Low risk / Some concerns / High risk / No information.

**Overall judgment:** Worst-of across the five domains, with adjustment for whether deviations affect the trial's primary inference.

## Per-study RoB 2 pilot

### Study 1 — Moel 2025 (PEARL trial)

**Design.** RCT, placebo-controlled, ~80 adults, one-year duration, primary cardiometabolic endpoints (BMI principal in the AAA4 synthesis description).

#### Domain 1 — Randomization process

- **Pilot judgment:** Low risk (provisional).
- **Reasoning.** The PEARL trial was registered (likely on ClinicalTrials.gov; verify NCT). Standard randomization procedure for a single-site geroscience RCT typically uses computer-generated allocation with concealed envelopes or web-based randomization. Baseline balance was reported in the AAA4 paper's source extraction (the "35.1% no change in BMI" baseline figure suggests a per-arm summary).
- **Data needed for source-text confirmation:**
  - Method of sequence generation.
  - Method of allocation concealment.
  - Baseline imbalance assessment (per-arm summary statistics with 95% CIs).
  - Whether stratification was used (frailty, age, baseline BMI).

#### Domain 2 — Deviations from intended interventions

- **Pilot judgment:** Some concerns (provisional).
- **Reasoning.** A 12-month rapamycin RCT in older adults has substantial scope for protocol deviation. Self-administered weekly oral dosing typically has imperfect adherence. The trial's null primary endpoint (BMI) raises the question of whether per-protocol vs intention-to-treat analysis differed materially. Without source-text reading of the adherence data, "some concerns" is the appropriate provisional judgment.
- **Data needed:**
  - Adherence rate per arm (pill count, blood concentration).
  - Per-protocol vs ITT analyses for primary endpoint.
  - Treatment-effect estimates under each.
  - Whether placebo-arm participants used off-protocol rapamycin (a real risk in the geroscience community).

#### Domain 3 — Missing outcome data

- **Pilot judgment:** Some concerns (provisional).
- **Reasoning.** Twelve-month trials in older adults typically experience 15–25% attrition (the AAA4 paper cites Schulz 2010's ~20% benchmark). PEARL's n ≈ 80 means even moderate attrition produces statistical-power loss. Without source-text reporting of attrition reasons and sensitivity analyses, "some concerns" is appropriate.
- **Data needed:**
  - Attrition rate per arm with reasons.
  - Whether missingness was related to outcome (biased loss to follow-up).
  - Multiple imputation or other sensitivity analyses.

#### Domain 4 — Measurement of the outcome

- **Pilot judgment:** Low risk (provisional).
- **Reasoning.** BMI is an objective measurement with high inter-rater reliability; cardiometabolic markers (HbA1c, lipids, blood pressure) are similarly objective. Outcome assessor blinding is less critical for BMI than for subjective endpoints, though it remains good practice.
- **Data needed:**
  - Whether outcome assessors were blinded to allocation.
  - Whether all participants used the same measurement protocol (calibrated scales, fasting blood draws).
  - Whether the BMI measurement protocol matched the pre-registered protocol.

#### Domain 5 — Selection of the reported result

- **Pilot judgment:** Some concerns (provisional).
- **Reasoning.** The trial reports a *placebo-arm* statistic (35.1% no change in BMI) as a load-bearing finding in the AAA4 synthesis. This is unusual: typically RCT primary results are reported as treatment-vs-placebo differences, not within-placebo summaries. Without source-text inspection of the pre-registered analysis plan, the framing raises the question of whether the published analysis matches the pre-specified analysis. Treat as "some concerns" pending verification.
- **Data needed:**
  - Pre-registered statistical analysis plan (PROSPERO or trial registry).
  - Comparison of pre-registered vs reported analyses.
  - Whether subgroup analyses were pre-specified.

#### Overall judgment for Moel 2025

- **Provisional overall:** Some concerns.
- **Rationale:** Domain 2 (deviations), Domain 3 (missing data), and Domain 5 (selective reporting) all flag at "some concerns" pending source-text verification. The trial's load-bearing-null finding on BMI is internally consistent with the synthesis but unexamined at the protocol-adherence and analysis-plan level.

### Study 2 — Stanfield 2026 (RAPA-EX-01)

**Design.** RCT, double-blind, placebo-controlled, older adults, weekly sirolimus + structured exercise vs placebo + exercise. Multiple cardiometabolic endpoints reported (HbA1c with bidirectional p-values).

#### Domain 1 — Randomization process

- **Pilot judgment:** Low risk (provisional).
- **Reasoning.** "Double-blind, placebo-controlled" framing in the AAA4 synthesis suggests competent randomization. The design is similar to PEARL in the relevant respects.
- **Data needed:** Same as Moel 2025.

#### Domain 2 — Deviations from intended interventions

- **Pilot judgment:** Some concerns to High risk (provisional).
- **Reasoning.** RAPA-EX-01 is a *combination* trial: rapamycin + structured exercise. The exercise component introduces additional protocol-deviation risks (adherence to the exercise protocol, supervised vs unsupervised sessions, equivalence of exercise across arms). The bidirectional HbA1c result raises specific concerns about per-protocol heterogeneity — the placebo-arm "increase in HbA1c (p = 0.012)" is an unusual finding that might reflect non-adherence or co-intervention crossover.
- **Data needed:**
  - Exercise-protocol adherence per arm (session attendance, intensity).
  - Rapamycin adherence per arm (pill count, blood concentration).
  - Whether adherence rates were balanced across arms.
  - Sensitivity analyses excluding non-adherent participants.

#### Domain 3 — Missing outcome data

- **Pilot judgment:** Some concerns (provisional).
- **Reasoning.** Older-adult RCTs combined with structured exercise typically have higher attrition than placebo-only trials, due to the burden of the exercise protocol. Without source-text reporting, "some concerns" is appropriate.

#### Domain 4 — Measurement of the outcome

- **Pilot judgment:** Low risk (provisional).
- **Reasoning.** HbA1c is a well-standardised, objective outcome with high analytical reliability. Outcome assessor blinding is straightforward in a double-blind design.

#### Domain 5 — Selection of the reported result

- **Pilot judgment:** High risk (provisional).
- **Reasoning.** The reported HbA1c results in the AAA4 synthesis are *multiple p-values across multiple comparisons* (p < 0.001, p = 0.025, p = 0.012, p = 0.036, p = 0.030), with both decreases and increases in the placebo arm. This pattern is consistent with multiple-comparison correction not being applied or with subgroup-by-time-point analyses being reported selectively. The bidirectional placebo-arm signal is particularly concerning: it suggests either (a) a genuine biological pattern, (b) chance, or (c) selective reporting of a subset of comparisons. Without source-text inspection of the pre-registered analysis plan, the multiple-p-value pattern raises substantial risk of selective reporting.
- **Data needed:**
  - Pre-registered statistical analysis plan.
  - Number of comparisons performed vs reported.
  - Multiple-comparison correction (Bonferroni, FDR, hierarchical testing).
  - Whether subgroup analyses (e.g., by age, baseline HbA1c, exercise adherence) were pre-specified.

#### Overall judgment for Stanfield 2026

- **Provisional overall:** Some concerns to High risk, depending on resolution of Domain 5.
- **Rationale:** The multiple-p-value bidirectional pattern is the load-bearing concern. If the analysis plan was pre-registered with a specific primary endpoint and the bidirectional results are a within-trial finding from pre-specified analyses, the trial drops to "some concerns." If the pattern reflects selective reporting from a larger analysis space, it is "high risk."

### Study 3 — Kell 2026 (observational immune cohort) — ROBINS-I

**Design.** Observational cohort, no randomization. Indirectness flagged in the AAA4 synthesis. Primary readout: PBMC mTOR signaling reduction at five weeks.

#### ROBINS-I framework (different from RoB 2)

ROBINS-I has seven domains:
1. Confounding
2. Selection of participants
3. Classification of interventions
4. Deviations from intended interventions
5. Missing data
6. Measurement of outcomes
7. Selection of the reported result

#### Pilot judgment per domain (provisional)

| Domain | Judgment | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Confounding | Serious | Observational design; participants self-selected for rapamycin use; baseline differences from non-users likely confound the mTOR-signaling finding. |
| 2. Selection of participants | Moderate | Cohort-selection criteria not described in AAA4 synthesis; risk of selection by indication. |
| 3. Classification of interventions | Low | Rapamycin exposure is verifiable (prescription records, blood levels). |
| 4. Deviations from intended interventions | Low to Moderate | Self-selected dosing regimens introduce variability. |
| 5. Missing data | Moderate | Five-week follow-up is short; loss to follow-up likely low but unspecified. |
| 6. Measurement of outcomes | Moderate | mTOR signaling and DNA-damage markers are well-validated assays; outcome ascertainment is largely unbiased *within-participant* (longitudinal) but vulnerable to batch effects. |
| 7. Selection of the reported result | Moderate to Serious | Observational cohorts typically allow many post-hoc analyses; pre-registration unlikely; selective reporting is a structural risk. |

#### Overall judgment for Kell 2026

- **Provisional overall (ROBINS-I):** Serious.
- **Rationale:** Confounding is the dominant concern; observational rapamycin users self-select by health status, financial means, and access to off-label prescribing. Without strong confounder adjustment, the five-week mTOR-signaling reduction cannot be causally attributed to rapamycin per se.

### Study 5 — Mannick 2018 (PIE trial / RTB101) — provisional, conditional on corpus expansion

**Design.** RCT, double-blind, placebo-controlled, ~264 older adults, RTB101 ± everolimus, primary endpoint respiratory tract infection rate over 16 weeks.

*Currently absent from corpus; if corpus expansion lane surfaces this paper, RoB 2 should be applied.*

#### Provisional judgment

The PIE trial is a stronger design than Mannick 2014 in several respects (larger n, clinical incidence endpoint, multiple-comparison-aware primary analysis). The primary risk-of-bias concern is the same Domain 5 issue: industry sponsorship and the company's subsequent phase 3 negative result raise questions about how the field has updated on the PIE finding.

- **Provisional overall:** Some concerns.
- **Reasoning:** The phase 3 negative result (Mannick 2021 / PROTECTOR) effectively re-reads PIE 2018 as a positive phase 2 that did not replicate at scale. This is not strictly a RoB 2 concern (which evaluates the trial's internal validity), but it bears on how the trial should be weighted in synthesis.

SYRCLE has 10 domains based on RoB 2 adapted for animal studies. Key domains:

2. Baseline characteristics
3. Allocation concealment
4. Random housing
5. Blinding of caregivers
6. Random outcome assessment
7. Blinding of outcome assessor
8. Incomplete outcome data

## Source-text extraction fields the bot must capture

Per RCT, the extraction pipeline must extract the following fields from source text before automated RoB 2 application is credible:

| Field | RoB 2 domain | Source location |
|---|---|---|
| Method of sequence generation | 1 | Methods → Randomization |
| Method of allocation concealment | 1 | Methods → Randomization |
| Pre-registered analysis plan link / NCT | 5 | Methods → Trial registration |
| Pre-specified primary endpoint | 5 | Methods → Outcomes |
| Adherence rate per arm | 2 | Results → Participants / Follow-up |
| Adherence measurement method (pill count / blood concentration) | 2 | Methods → Adherence |
| ITT vs PP analysis comparison | 2 | Results → Primary analysis |
| Attrition rate per arm | 3 | CONSORT flow diagram |
| Reasons for attrition | 3 | Results → Participants |
| Missing-data sensitivity analysis | 3 | Methods → Statistical analysis |
| Outcome assessor blinding | 4 | Methods → Outcome assessment |
| Pre-registered analysis vs reported analysis comparison | 5 | Methods + Results comparison |
| Number of comparisons performed vs reported | 5 | Results section enumeration |
| Multiple-comparison correction method | 5 | Methods → Statistical analysis |

These fields are extractable by an LLM extractor with a structured schema, but require source-text access (PDF or HTML) and a per-paper prompt that instructs the extractor to mark fields as "not reported" rather than synthesising from context.

## Activation plan for `scripts/risk_of_bias.py`

The current scaffold derives RoB labels from tier × directness rollup. To activate source-text RoB 2:

1. **Add a `rob_extraction` schema** with the 14 fields above per RCT.
2. **Run a per-paper extraction call** with a RoB-2-tuned prompt (one call per paper per domain, or one call per paper for all domains with structured JSON output).
3. **Apply RoB 2 signaling-question logic** deterministically to the extracted fields → domain-level judgment → overall judgment.
4. **Persist** the extraction + judgment into the bundle alongside `quant_claims`.
5. **Replace** the tier-rollup Table 4 with the source-text RoB 2 / ROBINS-I / SYRCLE table.
6. **Audit-gate**: the AAA-CLIN cert must require source-text RoB 2 for ≥80% of receipts before declaring a "Cochrane-grade systematic review" framing.

This is approximately 200–300 LOC of pipeline code plus a per-paper prompt of ~1,500 tokens.

## What this pilot refuses to claim

- That my provisional judgments are correct without source-text verification. They are reasoning from synthesis description and field knowledge, not from protocol reading.
- That the post-rescue 34-receipt corpus, once it has all Tier-1 papers (per `missing_literature_audit.md`), will produce identical judgments. Each new paper requires its own RoB 2 pass.
- That tier × directness is a *defensible* RoB rollup. It is not. The current Table 4 is operationally equivalent to "no RoB assessment was performed"; the paper's claim of "supplemental Cochrane RoB-2" is misleading and should be revised in the next render.

## Recommended changes to the AAA4 paper before next render

Even before source-text RoB 2 is automated, the paper should:

1. **Remove the "Cochrane RoB-2 / ROBINS-I" naming from Table 4** unless the source-text pass has run. The current rollup is a tier-directness derivation and should be labelled as such.
2. **Add a note** explaining that source-text RoB 2 is on the activation roadmap and citing this manual pilot as the methodological scaffold.
3. **For each receipt, add a "data needed for RoB 2" line** in the supplement listing the 14 extraction fields above.

These changes lower the desk-rejection risk by removing the misleading framing without claiming capability the pipeline does not yet have.

## Falsifying conditions

This pilot is wrong if:

1. **My provisional judgments diverge materially from a source-text pass** by an experienced systematic-review methodologist. Mitigation: use this pilot as a starting matrix, not a finished assessment; have the next render's source-text RoB 2 pass override every judgment.
2. **The trials I have characterised do not match their actual designs** — e.g., if PEARL was not double-blind, or if RAPA-EX-01's primary endpoint was not HbA1c. Mitigation: each provisional judgment is conditional on the AAA4 synthesis description; verify before relying.
3. **The 14-field extraction schema misses domain-relevant data** — e.g., if specific SYRCLE concerns about batch-housing in Harrison 2009 are not captured. Mitigation: schema is extensible; add fields as the source-text pass surfaces gaps.
