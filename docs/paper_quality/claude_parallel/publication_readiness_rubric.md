## How to read this rubric

Four publication tiers:

1. **bioRxiv-ready** — preprint quality. Methodologically defensible; not desk-rejected at any reasonable venue. Does not yet engage the field at peer-review depth.
2. **Mid-tier journal-ready** — passes peer review at venues like *GeroScience*, *Mechanisms of Ageing and Development*, *Ageing Research Reviews*, *Aging* (Albany), *Journal of Gerontology* with modest revisions. Impact factor roughly 3–6.
3. ***Aging Cell*-ready** — top specialty aging journal (~IF 8–10). Requires field-aware framework engagement, formal RoB 2 + GRADE, novel methodological or analytical contribution.
4. ***Nature Aging*-ready** — top general aging journal (~IF 15–18). Requires definitive synthesis OR methodology contribution that other specialty venues will cite as reference. The paper must be the *expected reference* for the topic for ≥18 months.

Each tier has five dimensions:
- **Corpus** — coverage and depth.
- **Methodology** — RoB 2, GRADE, comparable-effect audit, screening protocol.
- **Framework engagement** — named field positions, novel organising scaffold.
- **Novelty** — distinctive contribution that prior reviews have not made.
- **Prose** — manuscript-grade language, no template tells, clear narrative.

Each dimension scored Pass / Marginal / Fail per tier.

## What separates each tier

**Tier 1 → Tier 2:** Corpus expansion (10 → 20 receipts) + GRADE + RoB 2 (or honest relabel) + Mannick/Lamming/López-Otín engagement.

**Tier 2 → Tier 3:** Corpus completeness (Tier-1 paper coverage) + novel organising framework with falsifying conditions + selective meta-analysis where genuinely poolable + manuscript-facing Methods.

**Tier 3 → Tier 4:** Dual-screener equivalent + PROSPERO-style pre-registration + agenda-setting Discussion + corpus depth (40+ receipts).

The deltas are concrete and ordered: a paper that lands at Tier 2 needs three specific additions to reach Tier 3; a paper at Tier 3 needs four specific additions to reach Tier 4.

## What this rubric refuses to claim

- That my tier definitions are universal across journals. *Aging Cell* and *Aging* (Albany) are different journals with different bars; my rubric treats them as approximately equivalent for Tier 2 / Tier 3, but specific journal expectations vary.
- That post-publication impact predicts pre-submission tier. A Tier-3-ready paper may end up cited like a Tier-4 paper if it lands the right framework moment, or vice versa.
- That the "expected post-expansion" scores are guaranteed. They are conditional on the corpus expansion landing the Tier-1 papers and on the activation plans (RoB 2, GRADE, inferential bridge) succeeding. If any of these fail, the scoring drops.

## Falsifying conditions

This rubric is wrong if:

1. **A Tier-2 venue desk-rejects a paper that meets all Tier-2 criteria.** Mitigation: cross-check with recent acceptance patterns at the named venues.
2. **A Tier-4 venue accepts a paper that is below Tier-4 by these criteria.** This happens occasionally with high-profile authors; the rubric tracks the *median* expectation, not the ceiling.
3. **My corpus-size benchmarks (10 / 20 / 30 / 40) are off by significant margins.** Recent rapamycin reviews at the named venues should be checked for actual receipt counts; if Lee 2024 had only 19 receipts, my Tier-3 / Tier-4 benchmarks may be too high.
